# Antiviral Therapeutics

An encyclopaedic, open-access reference on antiviral therapeutics across the human virome — molecular mechanism, clinical virology and bedside decision-making — for medical practitioners with an interest in infectious diseases.

**Authors:** Saranga Sumathipala and Claude (Anthropic) · **Edition:** Reference edition, 2026 · **Format:** PDF, 69 pages

**[Download the PDF](Antiviral_Therapeutics.pdf)** · **[Web page](https://gsumathipala.github.io/antiviral-therapeutics/)**

## How it is written

Every virus chapter is written at three altitudes, marked in the text:

- **Molecular virology** — genome class and replication strategy, the precise enzymatic or structural step inhibited, the biochemistry of inhibition, why the drug is selective for virus over host, and the structural basis of escape.
- **Clinical virology** — which assay on which specimen reported in which units, how to read a viral load trajectory, when and how to order genotypic or phenotypic resistance testing, minority-variant thresholds, therapeutic drug monitoring and the pre-analytical traps.
- **Bedside** — indication, regimen, dose, route, duration, renal and hepatic adjustment, interactions, toxicity monitoring, and what to do when first-line therapy fails.

Recommendations carry **IDSA/USPHS two-part evidence grades** (A–E for strength of recommendation, I–III for quality of evidence), with an explicit calibration warning that most inpatient antiviral practice rests at B-II or C-III rather than A-I.

## Contents

**Part I — Foundations:** the druggable virus and the Baltimore target map; molecular pharmacology (activation cascades, ProTides, phosphonates, chain-termination modes, intracellular half-lives, inhibitory quotient, sanctuary compartments); resistance as population genetics (mutation supply, genetic barrier, mechanistic classes, pseudoresistance); **resistance testing from plaque reduction to long-read NGS**, with the scientific principle behind each method; viral load measurement and IU/mL pitfalls; dose adjustment in organ dysfunction, CRRT, ECMO, pregnancy and childhood; and antiviral stewardship.

**Part II — The human virome, virus by virus:** herpesviruses (HSV, VZV, CMV, EBV, HHV-6/7/8, B virus); hepatitis A, B, C, D and E; HIV-1, HIV-2 and HTLV; influenza; coronaviruses; RSV, metapneumovirus, parainfluenza, measles, mumps, rubella and the henipaviruses; enteroviruses, rhinoviruses and parechoviruses; adenovirus, BK and JC polyomaviruses, papillomaviruses, parvovirus B19 and anelloviruses; poxviruses including mpox; rabies and the arboviruses; viral haemorrhagic fevers; and viral gastroenteritis.

**Part III — Cross-cutting practice:** drug interactions, toxicity and monitoring, strategy in the immunocompromised host, and where the field is going.

**Part IV — Appendices:** an antiviral spectrum matrix; **viruses for which there is no effective antiviral**, with what does change outcome; a resistance-mutation quick reference; and abbreviations.

Ten original figures: the replication cycle as an intervention map, activation cascades and the locus of selectivity, six decades of resistance testing, and target maps for herpesviruses, CMV, HBV, HCV, influenza, HIV and the poxviruses.

## Disclaimer

For education only. Doses, thresholds, breakpoints and licensed indications change over time and differ between countries and centres. Antiviral susceptibility interpretation is assay- and reference-strain-dependent; no CLSI/EUCAST-equivalent universal breakpoint framework exists. Verify against current national product labelling, regional guidelines and local antimicrobial stewardship policy before prescribing.
